1. **Durable admission versus availability-first capture.** I retain durable admission and explicit backpressure. The requested fail-safe goal and retrospective capture depend on keeping already released observations; releasing frames after recorder failure knowingly gives that up. If the person prioritizes uninterrupted production instead, the guarantee must change rather than being described as no-loss.
2. **Universal imposed-prefix reconstruction.** I support imposed histories where the relevant state is reconstructible, including continuation prompt changes. I reject the claim that recorded model/tool outputs restore any harness. Hidden memory, local files, and in-flight operations are concrete counterexamples; a declared limitation is more useful than a universal promise disproved by the first file-writing tool.
3. **Unconditional repeated-read reuse.** An unchanged write history is not proof of stable output. Time-sensitive reads, cursor state, and changing recorded observations make generic last-response reuse unsound. Explicit stability rules preserve useful reuse without quietly inventing semantics.
4. **Core-owned entity maps.** Connector plugins should own state effects and derivation. A generic CRUD/filter language would become another connector abstraction to maintain while still failing on recurrence, permissions, and ranking. Keeping the core’s answer chain small better serves the spec’s replaceable-parts goal.
5. **Recovery by starting fresh after prefix drift.** Durable operation ownership and recoverable connector transitions are necessary implementation mechanisms, not extra product features. Retaining evidence and reporting unreconstructible work is preferable to silently substituting a new stochastic execution and treating its first completed outcome as the original sample.
6. **Adjacent checksum locks and mandatory git workspaces.** Checks require approval authority outside the Improver’s writable boundary; checksums alone cannot enforce that. Harness identity can be content-based, and allowed new files must be supported. Git is valuable for exporting fixes, but it should not narrow who can use Phoenix.
